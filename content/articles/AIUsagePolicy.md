*This is a living document that is liable to change. Due to the rapidly changing nature of this technology I cannot say this will be my policy forever. I do commit that any amendments will be clearly discussed and public in this document. If you are attempting to do creative work in our modern day, I urge you to be forthwith and public with your use of AI generative technology.* 

## My Personal AI Usage Policy 

### Creative Writing: 

**I will not personally use AI generative technology to write, edit, review or transform any creative writing attributed to me.** 

*Running Notes:* 
- Creative writing is my choice of artistic expression. I do not want AI anywhere near that. 
- Creative writing here includes any fiction or non-fiction work that is creative in nature; novels, scripts, articles (including this blog), essays, campaign settings, TTRPG adventures etc. 
- Creative writing does **not** include things like code, documentation, specifications, manuals etc. 
- Unfortunately, I cannot ensure that other third parties (editors, publishers, submission portals etc.) will not attempt to use AI to edit/review my work.

### Art Commissions:

**I will not personally commission AI generative technology to create illustrations, video or music in any capacity (professionally or personally) for any reason beyond one-off personal jokes. I will always prioritise the opportunity to pay a human artist for work over an AI company.**

*Running Notes*:
- [Matt Rhodes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3eR3UlXwU_k) (an amazing artist) reframed 'AI Art' for me when he said: 
    > There is no such thing as an 'AI Artist'. [AI is] not a tool to create art, but a *platform* for commissioning art... At no point can you say 'look at what I made', you can only say 'look at what I *had* made'. You commissioned it.
- I think this framing is important in two ways: 
	1. It denounces use of AI as a creative act. You are not creating, you are commissioning.
	2. The term 'commissioning' evokes ideas of patronage to me. I personally want to patron the creativity of human artists not bolster the coffers of some AI company. 

### Coding:

**I will make liberal use of AI coding agents due to my work as a Software Engineer. This includes generating code, documentation, tests and specifications. I try to champion software quality where I can.**

*Running Notes:*
- This is where most of my AI usage is centred. It is nearly impossible nowadays to avoid using AI as a working Software Engineer in a corporate company. 
- It's my belief that the widespread 'F_ck it, Ship it' mentality in tech has led to poor software quality standards across the industry. AI-assisted bad actors have started to take advantage of this lack of quality. AI tooling also has the potential to plug these deficiencies if used correctly by talented and knowledgable developers. However, I currently see the opposite being marketed; not a specialised tool to multiply the capabilities of experienced engineers, but instead a god-machine freely bestowing [monkey paws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Monkey's_Paw) on a unsuspecting laymen public.

### Research: 

**I will attempt to prioritise primary texts and trusted sources over AI summaries. I may use AI for interactive basic research but nothing more than what I would do on Wikipedia or Google Scholar.**

*Running Notes:*
- This is a hard one for me to pin down, and judging by [Hank Green's recent controversy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ssE1rmt3EpE) I'm not the only one. This is also the one I see changing most in the future.
- AI is obviously good at research. For example, it has made some large breakthroughs in [math, physics and computer science](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/) recently. However, I'm not doing that type of research. Mostly it is for my intellectual enjoyment and understanding. For that use case, I'm not sure that AI is a good fit. It is too quick and dopinaminergic to forge those [slow processes that forge deep understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking,_Fast_and_Slow) and too sycophantic for my tastes to have proper critical debates. I will stick to primary sources if my goal is depth. 

### Other Details:

I classify 'AI generative technology' widely as:
- LLM Chatbots: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.
- Diffusion Models: Sora, Higgsfield, OpenArt, Midjourney, etc. 
- AI Agents: Codex, Muse, Dots, GrokBot, etc. 
- Built in AI Toolsets: Grammarly, Notion, Canva AI, Firefly, etc.
